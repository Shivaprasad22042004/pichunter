⚙ How it works

You do not need to understand any of this to use the site. But here is what actually happens, from the moment you click the button.

Step 1 — We find your face

The server takes your photo and looks for a face in it. If it finds more than one, it uses the largest one. If the face is too small to read properly, it stops and asks for a better photo rather than guessing.

Under the bonnet: a face detector called SCRFD scans the image at several sizes at once, so it catches faces that are large and small.

Step 2 — Your face becomes a list of numbers

This is the clever part. The face is straightened (turned upright, scaled to a standard size) and then squeezed into a list of 512 numbers. Those numbers describe the shape of your face — the distance between your eyes, the curve of your jaw, and so on.

Two photos of the same person produce very similar lists. Two different people produce very different lists. Crucially, the numbers do not contain your photo. You cannot turn them back into a picture.

Under the bonnet: this is an ArcFace model (w600k_r50), which learned what faces look like from millions of examples.

Step 3 — We compare against everyone in the archive

Every single face in the ten thousand photos was prepared the same way in advance, once. That is why the search is fast: the hard work happened earlier.

When you search, your list of numbers is compared against all 82,620 faces that were found in the archive. Each comparison gives a score from 0 to 1.

ScoreWhat it means
0.95 – 1.00Almost certainly the same person
0.50 – 0.95Very likely you — shown as a normal match
0.42 – 0.50Maybe you — shown as "possible" so you can check
below 0.42Not shown. This is where strangers usually land.

Step 4 — You get file names

Any photo where at least one face scored above the cut-off is listed, with the folder it lives in. A photo is only listed once even if several faces matched.

How the archive was prepared

This is the part that took the time, and it only happens once.

WhatNumber
Photos scanned9,960
Faces found115,460
Faces kept (big enough to be useful)82,620
Time it took, on one laptopabout 2 hours

Faces smaller than 20 pixels wide were discarded. They are unreadable by humans too — keeping them added no matches, only noise.

Why a search takes about 15 seconds

The site runs on a free server, which is intentionally slow (about a tenth of a normal computer core). Comparing your face against 82,620 others is quick; getting the models loaded and running on that little CPU is what takes the time.

On a normal laptop the same search takes under one second.

What it cannot do

  • Follow you through time. Different day, different photo — the system does not and cannot build a profile of you.
  • Find you in photos you are not in. No match beats a wrong match.
  • See through masks, sunglasses or heavy shadow.
  • Recognise you from behind or from the side. It needs to see a reasonable amount of your face.
Does what I wear matter? No. The comparison uses only your face — it crops to your face before doing anything else, so a graduation gown does not help and does not hurt. What does help is a photo taken under similar conditions to the archive photos: same day, same kind of light, facing the camera.
